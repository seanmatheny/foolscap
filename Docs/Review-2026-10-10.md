# Foolscap review, 10 October 2026

Three questions were asked: a quick zoom for Scribe pages, whether the animations are still
120 Hz and why window drags feel choppy, and a structural and memory review after the run of big
features (Scribe spreads and the VLM engine, Highlights, Jira, Secrets). This note records what was
found, what was changed in this pass, and what is recommended next, in priority order.

## 1. Scribe page zoom (done)

`ScribePageZoom` lifts a page over the notebook area. Click a page's ink (a magnifier shows on
hover) or press Space for the page at the top of the view. Pinch, `=`/`-` or a double-click zoom
up to 4×; ← → step pages; ⎋, Space or a click beside the page put it back. Bitmaps come from
`ScribePageRenderer.image(persist: false)` in 100 pt steps, capped at 2400 px wide (the Scribe
draws 1860), kept in a separate 96 MB memory cache so stepping through a notebook at 4× never
evicts the spread. `--scribe=Folder/Notebook --scribe-zoom=N` opens page N zoomed for screenshots.
Geometry is pure (`ScribeZoomLayout`) and tested (`ScribeZoomTests`).

## 2. Animations and window drags

### What did not change

The page-curl pipeline, the opening animation, the flyleaf turn and the search palette's
transition have not changed since 27–29 September (`git log` on `PageCurl.swift`,
`PageCurlRenderer.swift`, `PageTurnOverlay.swift`, `OpeningAnimation.swift`, `WindowChrome.swift`).
The shader gained rounded corners (one `length()` per sample). The display is a 120 Hz ProMotion
panel and was running at 120 Hz when checked. SwiftUI's animations are Core Animation driven and
run at the display's rate; nothing in the app pins them lower.

### What did change around them

- **The Qwen3-VL engine is selected** (`scribeOCREngine = vlm`). Each sync that finds a changed
  page launches `scribe-vlm`, which maps 5.8 GB of weights and saturates the GPU for about 12 s a
  page; switching engines re-reads every notebook once. On an 18 GB machine that was already
  1.2 GB into swap, this is the only candidate that explains a choppy *window drag*: a drag is
  composited by the window server on the GPU with no app involvement, so only GPU contention or
  memory pressure can make it stutter. Highlights also launches the Kindle app hidden 5 s after
  launch, and Jira syncs at launch. Check: Activity Monitor's GPU history while it feels choppy,
  or whether the Scribe status line says "Reading …".
- **Hovering the leather band (the drag handle) re-rendered the whole notebook.**
  `NotebookView.body` read `WindowState.shared.trafficLightsShown`; every hover enter/exit
  rebuilt the cover, the tabs and `page(open)` → a fresh `AnyView(DailyNotesPage)` →
  `MarkdownEditor.updateNSView`, which built a new `EditorPalette` (ten fonts) and reassigned
  the header and footer hosting views' roots every time. Two 0.18 s animations started in the
  same instant as the drag. Opening the search palette had the same shape through `RootView`.
- **A tab change recomputed the window shadow in the first frames of the turn**
  (`invalidateShadow` on `shapeVersion`), while the window-server photograph, a new
  `NSWindow` for the curl and the new page were all being built.

### Changed in this pass

- `CoverStamp` and `FullScreenTopInset` read the window state themselves; the notebook body no
  longer does.
- `SearchOverlay` observes the search coordinator; `RootView` no longer re-renders on open/close.
- `MarkdownEditor.updateNSView` rebuilds the palette and the hosted header/footer only when the
  theme changed or a header/footer came or went (the day-pages providers are in the editor's id).
- The shadow recompute waits 0.65 s, until the turn has ended.
- The curl is now driven by a `CAMetalDisplayLink` on its own thread with
  `preferredFrameRateRange` 80–120. Measured with `FOOLSCAP_TURN_LOG=1`: the old `nextDrawable`
  loop gave 37–49 frames a turn (60–80 Hz; a main-thread `CADisplayLink` hint did not change it),
  the Metal display link gives 65–66 frames in 548 ms, 120 Hz, on every one of six turns.
- The photograph's byte check moved to the curl thread: it copied the 28 MB image on the main
  thread, 30 ms of every turn. Main thread before the page swap is now 18–37 ms (was 105–112).
- `scribe-vlm`, `scribe-ocr` and `calibre-debug` run at `.utility` QoS.
- Both helpers exit when Foolscap dies (poll `getppid`), and quitting Foolscap stops a sync or
  import under way, so a 6 GB helper never outlives the app.

### Recommended next

1. Gate handwriting recognition on idleness (no key/mouse event for 30 s, or the app not
   frontmost), cap pages per pass, and after an engine switch re-read notebooks lazily (the one
   opened first) instead of every notebook at once. Show "reading handwriting" in the status
   line so the cause is visible when the machine slows.
2. Defer the hidden Kindle launch to idle rather than 5 s after launch; stagger it after the
   Scribe startup pass; never run the VLM, Calibre and Kindle together (a one-slot gate).
3. Page-turn start-up: reuse one `PageTurnWindow` instead of making an `NSWindow` per turn
   (the first turn after launch still pays ~40 ms for the window server's first capture).
4. `HighlightMark`'s `.blendMode(.multiply)` on every Today row could be a flat pre-multiplied
   colour on the known paper colour: one compositing group fewer per row.
5. Fold chevrons: `Folding.swift` hit-tests TextKit on every `mouseMoved`; cache the hovered
   line's band and skip the test while the pointer stays inside it.
6. If drags still stutter with nothing running: on `NSWindow.willMoveNotification` set
   `hasShadow = false` and restore on `didMoveNotification` to confirm the shaped shadow is
   the cost (the caret blink during a drag dirties the window).

## 3. Memory

The app idles at 230–290 MB. The machine has 18 GB with 1.2 GB in swap, so the targets are peaks
and churn rather than the idle figure.

### Changed in this pass

- The editor's decoded-image cache is one shared 96 MB `NSCache` instead of 256 MB per editor
  (a day switch makes a new editor, so the per-editor cache both re-decoded and multiplied).
- Highlights cover thumbnails and link-preview metadata moved from unbounded dictionaries to
  `NSCache` (24 MB; 64 entries), which also empties under memory pressure.
- Scribe spread cache 256 → 128 MB; zoomed pages in their own 96 MB cache; PNG encodes run on
  one serial queue (a fast scroll queued dozens, each holding a 5 MB bitmap); the Daily page's
  appendix thumbnails close the PDF they opened (PDFKit keeps render caches per open document).

### Recommended next, in order

1. **Note images are decoded at 1800 px and every one in the note is held by its
   `NSImageView`** regardless of whether it is on screen (`OverlayController.thumbnail`,
   `maxThumbnailPixels`). Decode at display size (column width × backing scale, 320 pt tall by
   default), re-decode larger only when a picture is dragged wider, and drop `imageView.image`
   for overlays far outside `visibleRect` in `reposition()`. A screenshot-heavy day is
   250–400 MB today.
2. **`NotebookLibrary.documents` is pruned only when the Daily page changes day.** The
   Highlights importer, Jira and the task providers open documents that then stay resident
   (text storage plus a `BlockMap` copying every line). Prune after saves from the library, or
   give write-only callers a transient document.
3. **Two full `BlockMap` scans per keystroke** (`NoteDocument.didEdit` and
   `MarkdownStyler.didProcessEditing`), each allocating a `String` per line. Make the
   document's map lazy or hand the styler's map back; mark it `@ObservationIgnored`.
4. **One autosave fans out into full reloads of every section** (`changes` carries no paths):
   Highlights re-reads its whole table and stats every cover, the aggregator re-queries every
   provider (and `DailyNotesTaskProvider.tasks()` saves again first). Carry the changed paths
   and let listeners filter.
5. `ScribeDayPagesProvider` parses and caches every notebook's whole transcript to answer
   "which pages are dated today"; cache a day → pages index keyed on `transcribedHash` instead.
6. `VLMOCRRunner.hashPages` renders every page at 1860 × 2480 in the app on every recognise
   call even when all pages are cached; skip when the OCR cache already covers the notebook's
   `contentHash`.
7. `KindleApp.terminate()`'s result is ignored; retry then `forceTerminate` so a Kindle showing
   a dialog does not stay resident.
8. `WindowChrome` adds three observers per `ChromeView` and never removes them (harmless with
   one window; leaks three blocks per extra window).

## 4. Structure

### Verdict

The architecture holds: every target imports only what `Package.swift` declares, the section
protocol lives in Core with optional capabilities, dependency injection is uniform and the tests
use it (202 tests in 28 files, strong on parsers, engines with fakes, store and editor
commands). Actors own long work, `@MainActor @Observable` sections own UI state, blocking IO goes
through `Task.detached`. Nothing is broken; the findings are duplication from four sections
written in quick succession.

### Changed in this pass

`PlaceholderSection`/`PlaceholderPage`, `PageRuling.ruleOffset(theme:)` and
`PeriodicScheduler.isRunning` (all unused) removed; four stale comments fixed (`#today` tag,
"Scribe will be another", "platform-neutral" Core).

### Recommended refactors, in order

1. **Optional-section descriptor table** (medium). `AppModel`'s four `setXEnabled` methods, the
   forced flags in init, the defaults observer and `reloadAfterRestore` are the same code four
   times; `PreferencesView` hard-codes the four tabs by id. A `[OptionalSectionDescriptor]`
   (id, enabled key, forcing flags, placement, index flag, settings tab, factory) in
   FoolscapSections collapses them to one `setEnabled(id:on:)`, makes a fifth section a one-entry
   addition, and puts the logic where a test can reach it. Do it before the next optional
   section, not as a chore.
2. **Move `PageRuling` to FoolscapUI and add a `RuledPage` scaffold** (small-medium). Jira and
   Scribe depend on the whole TextKit editor module only for `PageRuling`/`EditorPalette`
   baseline geometry; Tasks, Jira and Secrets repeat the same `GeometryReader > ScrollView >
   ZStack { RulingView … }` scaffold. `ruleOffset(headingFont:pitch:)` needs no palette.
3. **One `ProcessRunner`** (small). `HelperProcess` and `KFXExtractor.runProcess` are ~60
   identical lines of the subtlest code in the app (pipe draining, watchdog, lock-guarded
   cancellation, `nonisolated(unsafe)` captures).
4. **`VersionedStateFile`** (small). `ScribeState`, `JiraState`, `HighlightsState` and
   `DailyPickHistory` have byte-identical load/save; `ScribePaths` builds the Application
   Support path by hand instead of `AppSupport.directory`.
5. **File splits, no behaviour change**: `OverlayController.swift` into controller, link card
   and image view; `Backup.swift` into archive and manager; `TasksSection.swift` into model
   plus `UI/`; `KindleWatcher` and the import schedule out of `HighlightsSection.swift`;
   `MarkdownTextView.swift` into drawing, tag-completion, header/footer and paste extensions
   with `PageRuling`/`EditorFeatures` in their own files.
6. **`ScribeModelManager`** out of `ScribeSection` (the six `vlm*` properties and the download
   task), so the only code touching the helper binary and the 5 GB on disk is in one place.
7. **Async `DeviceKeyStore.privateKey`**: it blocks a cooperative-pool thread on a semaphore for
   the whole Touch ID sheet; `LAContext.evaluatePolicy` has an async form. Also `queue: .main`
   on `NoteDocument`'s text-storage observer, and `@MainActor` on `FoolscapUIResources.cache`.
8. **`SyncRunner`**: Scribe's and Jira's `startSync`/`cancelSync`/`performSync` with the `passID`
   guard, the `NWPathMonitor` reconnect and the three `statusText` cascades are near-copies.
   Worth it when the next syncing section arrives.
9. **Tests for silent-regression gaps**: `SearchCoordinator`/`DailyNotesSearchProvider`,
   `SecretsSection` edit flow and filters, `NotebookLibrary.externalChange` and conflicts,
   `BackupManager.nextAutomaticBackup`/`prune`, the section registry once it exists.

### Looked at and judged fine

Module graph; `NotebookSection` and the theme key in Core; task write-back through the shared
`NoteDocument` with Scribe and Jira writing through sinks; the save and scan chains and byte-exact
paths; `Keychain`, `PeriodicScheduler`, `FileIO`/`AppSupport` shared from Store; the `passID`
guard; Highlights' debounced off-main search; `PageTurnWindow` lifetime (`finish()` runs on every
path, `playing` empties); `AutoLock` watches clicks and keys, not `mouseMoved`; no observer of
window moves; `FullScreenMenuBar`'s timer only in full screen; `TextureOverlay`'s tiles are
bounded; `SecretsVault` plaintext while unlocked is by design.
